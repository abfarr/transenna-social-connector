## Step 2 of 5: Read the sources, strongest first

Work through what they gave you in this order. Later sources fill gaps and never overwrite an earlier one without flagging the conflict.

**A. Brand style guide (if provided).** Treat it as the source of truth for colors, fonts, logo rules, tone of voice and banned words. Pull exact values. Leave out anything the kit doesn't need.

**B. Website.** Read the home, about, services or products, and contact pages, plus a couple of blog posts if there are any. Pull: what they offer (exact names), who it's for, how people buy or join, claims they make, recurring phrases, and the visual system (colors, type, photo style). Never copy website text as the client's own words; note phrases as voice evidence only.

**C. Existing social accounts.** You'll review these in step 3.

**D. Other examples and inspiration.** Use the client's own past material as voice and style evidence. Use other people's accounts only for level of ambition and structure, never for their colors, copy or branding.

**If there is no brand guide:** work out the brand from B, C and D. Sample colors from the logo and site, and mark every sampled hex as an estimate. Name fonts only when you can identify them from the site's code or the client confirms them; otherwise describe the style ("a thin high-contrast serif") and mark it [CONFIRM].

**If you can't open a source** (a login wall, a social platform blocking you, a dead link): say which one in one line and ask for screenshots or an export instead. Never guess at what it contains.

For every fact you collect, record where it came from: guide, website page, social post, client said, or estimate.

Don't show the client a long dump of everything you found. When you're done reading, call `get_onboarding_step` with step 3.
